Explicit bilevel surrogate with a numerically checked lower response
=================================================================

This implementation changes the lower optimization problem. It does NOT
certify the global optimum of full neural FlashSAC. That distinction is part
of the method definition, not a hidden assumption.

At a search stage t, freeze the current FlashSAC backbone, critic, covariance,
temperature and batch statistics. Theta specifies a feasible local full-hand
reference edit. Simulate the frozen base policy under that reference to
obtain its adaptation observations D_t(theta). Use fixed sampling rules and
common Gaussian action noise across candidates.

For each observation, let h_i(theta) be the frozen actor's final feature
vector, augmented by 1 for its mean bias. Let m_i be the base Gaussian mean
before tanh. Differentiate the frozen-critic SAC actor loss

  ell_i(m) = alpha log pi_m(a_i | o_i) - min(Q1(o_i,a_i), Q2(o_i,a_i))

through the sampled action and tanh, holding its noise and covariance fixed.
Call that gradient g_i. Set one scalar eta_theta for the whole candidate's
dataset: min(configured_teacher_step, target_bound / max_i,j |g_i,j|).
The target is Y_i=-eta_theta*g_i; this rescales rather than coordinate-clips
the gradient. H stacks the h_i rows. The explicit lower objective is

  G_t(theta,W) = ||H(theta) W - Y(theta)||_F^2/(2N)
                 + lambda ||W||_F^2/2,       lambda > 0.

Ignoring a W-independent constant and dividing by eta_theta gives a
linearization of the frozen-critic empirical actor loss, a quadratic trust
penalty on logit changes, and a ridge penalty on the head increment.
This is a proximal actor-improvement SURROGATE, not the nonlinear SAC loss.

The adapted controller is

  pi_theta,W(o): mean = base_mean(o) + h(o)^T W,
                action = action_scale * tanh(mean).

All 56 outputs (wrists AND fingers) are adapted. W is folded into the existing
unit-direction/gain mean head and mean bias so ordinary inference/checkpoints
use the selected controller without an external runtime adapter. Every real
effective head matrix is representable by unit columns and scalar gains;
the lower variable W itself is unconstrained.

The stage's bilevel problem is

  minimize_theta F_t(theta, W_t*(theta)),
  W_t*(theta) = argmin_W G_t(theta,W).

F_t runs full-horizon VOC0 physics with that candidate's OWN adapted
controller. In local_nominal mode it scores the shared failure-local interval
(error onset through failure + 1 second), plus CHORD-style normalized tolerance-band wrench-support error (weight 5),
missed and unintended support penalties (weight 1 each), and separately
scaled reference penalties. No contact-position distance enters ranking or
acceptance. Both-object success
is computed before averaging. Object/reference commands remain immutable.
Search/confirmation use solved no-edit controls, and a selected reference
is published together with its selected lower response.

PROPOSITION 1: UNIQUE LOWER SOLUTION

Write A=H^T H/N+lambda I and B=H^T Y/N. The gradient is AW-B and
the Hessian in vectorized W is I_actions tensor A. For any vector v,
v^T A v=||Hv||^2/N+lambda||v||^2 >= lambda||v||^2.
Thus G is lambda-strongly convex, has a unique minimizer, and

  W*=solve(A,B).

No assumption of strong convexity of the neural network or contact simulator
is made. Strong convexity holds because THIS lower objective is quadratic.

PROPOSITION 2: COMPUTABLE APPROXIMATION BOUNDS

For a computed W_hat let R=A W_hat-B. Since R=A(W_hat-W*),

  ||W_hat-W*||_F <= ||R||_F/lambda.
  G(W_hat)-G(W*) = .5 tr(R^T A^-1 R)
                 <= ||R||_F^2/(2lambda).

The solver checks these residual-based bounds. It also recomputes them for
the effective deployed float32 head after folding, refusing a response above
the configured tolerance. Bounds are analytical real-arithmetic bounds
evaluated numerically, not formally verified interval-arithmetic certificates.
Floating-point action/matrix multiplication may introduce additional rounding.

With 1/L gradient steps and L>=lambda_max(A), the lower iteration contracts:

  ||W_K-W*||_F <= (1-lambda/L)^K ||W_0-W*||_F.

This establishes convergence of the iterative approximation for this actual
lower objective. The production solver uses a linear solve and residual check
instead of assuming that a fixed training budget reaches an optimum.

PROPOSITION 3: RESPONSE DERIVATIVES AND UPPER APPROXIMATION

At differentiable data mappings, differentiating AW*=B gives

  dW*=solve(A, dB-dA W*).

The supplied JVP implements this identity; tests compare against independent
finite differences. The robot search currently uses response-aware function
evaluations, not differentiation through contact physics.

For a bounded measurable composed function J(theta)=F(theta,W*(theta)),
Gaussian smoothing J_sigma(theta)=E_u[J(theta+sigma*u)] with u~N(0,I)
has gradient E[(J(theta+sigma*u)-J(theta-sigma*u))*u/(2sigma)].
Thus the optional two-sided estimator includes lower-response dependence
without differentiating the simulator. With each value error bounded by e,
its gradient bias is at most e*sqrt(d)/sigma, by the triangle inequality
and E||u||<=sqrt(d). A bound on W alone is NOT a bound on physical cost;
that further step needs a justified Lipschitz bound in W.
This estimator is a tested numerical primitive, not the production upper
proposal rule or a claim of convergence of the full training loop.

The production upper approximation samples a finite candidate set, solves
each lower response, and compares against a matched adapted no-edit control.
In local_nominal mode every pair uses the original demonstration start and
one common scoring interval across all windows. It selects the lowest local
cost satisfying prefix/large-regression guards, with a 1e-4 minimum gain.
The optional legacy strict_robust mode retests up to four finalists on three
paired perturbed resets and requires every per-reset gate. Neither statement
covers an unsearched continuous reference domain.

If evaluated feasible-set costs differ uniformly from ideal costs by at most
e, exact finite-set minimization gives regret at most 2e (plus the 1e-4
acceptance deadband in local_nominal mode): apply the bound
once at the selected candidate, the estimated minimization inequality, and
once at the ideal finite-set minimizer. If the continuous composed objective
is L-Lipschitz and the candidate set is an h-cover, add L*h. Neither a small
covering radius in 56D nor a physical-cost Lipschitz constant is certified by
this implementation. Three reset tests do not supply a distribution-free
task-success certificate. Held-out testing remains necessary.

WHAT THESE PROOFS DO NOT ESTABLISH

They do not bound the gap between this proximal lower surrogate and the
globally optimal neural RL policy. Between search stages, regular FlashSAC
learning refreshes the base controller/critic; G_t changes. Therefore the
code does not claim convergence of that whole evolving loop to a stationary
point of one fixed global bilevel objective. Freezing the base throughout an
outer optimization experiment would remove that particular source of drift,
but still would not turn the convex surrogate into full neural RL.

The method is explicit bilevel optimization OF A STATED SURROGATE. Calling it
an exact solver or a proven approximation of the original full neural-RL
bilevel problem would exceed what has been established here.

CHORD UPPER OBJECTIVE REVISION (2026-10-14)

For each time/world/hand/object cell, let s_ref and s_sim be support vectors.
The squared band deviation is ||max((1-beta)s_ref-s_sim,0)||^2 +
||max(s_sim-(1+beta)s_ref,0)||^2, beta=0.1. We divide by reference support
energy, cap each active cell at 10, average active cells within each object,
and give objects equal weight. This uses the tolerance-band loss underlying
CHORD Eq.3, with explicit normalization for upper candidate scoring; it is
not the paper's exponential lower RL reward.

Missed support is the fraction of reference-active directions absent in the
simulation, averaged over reference-active cells. Unintended support counts
reference-inactive hand/object cells with simulated support, plus their mean
squared support energy (capped at 10). No fingertip-to-reference-contact
position errors are computed or gated in this experiment.

Upper F = position/0.03 + orientation/0.3 + 2*(1-joint_object_success)
        + 5*cws_band_error + missed_fraction
        + unintended_fraction + unintended_energy
        + 3*instability + geometric_edit_and_rate_penalties.
F is capped to [0,100]. In local_nominal mode pose, joint-success and wrench
terms use [error onset, failure+1s), clipped to the horizon. All candidates
share this interval even when their edit windows differ. No failure uses the
whole episode. Earlier prefix mean errors may increase by at most 2 mm and
0.01 rad; prefix p95 by 5 mm and 0.03 rad. Large-regression guards allow at
most +5 cm and +20 degrees in each object's mean pose error, measured both
over the whole episode and separately after the scoring interval. New
instability is rejected. No per-object first-failure-time or individual
wrench-component veto is applied. Acceptance requires local cost improvement
>1e-4 at the original start; there are no perturbed confirmation resets.
The 16 perturbed baseline probes still select the failure/window and do not
accept or reject candidates. These tolerances are engineering choices.
Legacy strict_robust retains its three-reset, per-object nonregression rules.
Both wrists and fingers can change; intended hand/object labels remain fixed.
Raw normalized wrench MSE is retained as a diagnostic, not an upper cost.

The lower quadratic, response folding, residual bounds, physics settings,
and adaptive failure-based window selection are unchanged. Changing F does
not remove the stated limitations on full neural-RL/global convergence.

Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00033v2#S3.SS1

Upper FlashSAC proposal ablation (2026-10-14)
------------------------------------------
The optional upper_proposal=flashsac selects a learned contextual Gaussian proposal
distribution instead of independent zero-mean Gaussian sampling. All candidate
lower solves, objective terms, paired validation and pair publication are unchanged.
A search round inserts terminal transitions (context, proposed correction, reward),
where reward = clip(F_t(no-edit,W*_t(no-edit)) - F_t(theta,W*_t(theta)), -5, 5).
Critic/actor updates learn this one-step entropy-regularized bandit. Replay is
refreshed each stage; networks persist, so old reward labels are never replayed
against a changed lower stage. The no-edit action is explicitly included. The
best evaluated feasible pair is selected with F, without clipping its gain
to the proposal learner's [-5,5] reward interval.

The local feasible reference map bounds edits in both proposal modes. The upper
actor uses an identity action transform and initial Gaussian std 0.5, matching
the independent sampler's initial support. The implemented 32 gradient steps
(16 actor updates) per batch are an approximation, without a global upper-policy
convergence claim. Changing the proposal mechanism leaves the exact per-candidate
lower optimum and residual bound valid. It does not turn the algorithm into
an implicit-gradient method or prove whole-loop convergence.
