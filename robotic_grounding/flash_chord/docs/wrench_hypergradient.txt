WRENCH HYPERGRADIENT ENGINE
=========================

Status: ready for a GPU smoke run after CPU validation, separate from
response_bilevel. Existing frozen jobs do not pick this up automatically.
No job restart is part of implementation.

ACTUAL ALGORITHM

1. The ordinary lower FlashSAC continues training with its configured tracking,
   physics and regularization rewards. Capture 32 worlds every four transitions
   into a 32,768-row auxiliary buffer, with reference version, frame, observation,
   action, critic context, next observation, done, and measured wrench-support
   cost. The version is owned by the buffer; mixing versions is rejected.
2. Every 501,760 environment transitions, roll out the current pair from the
   fixed start without VOC. These transitions also enter the auxiliary buffer.
   Detect failure and propose up to four earlier windows using the existing
   onset/threshold/history selector. The actor does not learn the window index.
3. Fit twin scalar discounted wrench-cost critics from replay. Lower actor
   deterministic actions supply TD targets; max(twin cost) is pessimistic.
   Hold out rows from the current critic fitting round and log their TD error.
   The outer evaluation batch comes specifically from unassisted rollouts.
4. For each window, find its feasible coefficient intervals using the actual
   joint, cumulative-offset and correction-speed bounds. Build a local reference
   Jacobian with finite differences (normalized radius at most 0.01) through
   that edit and Newton FK; use one-sided derivatives at active limits.
   Recompute reference-conditioned observation fields in JAX. Verify zero edit
   reproduces the stored observation. Measured state, old actions, object targets
   and desired support are constants. This is a local linearization; simulator
   trajectories and sampled-state distributions are NOT differentiated.
5. The upper MLP produces a bounded 56D wrist-and-finger correction. Starting
   from the incoming lower actor, take three differentiable projected SGD steps
   on the existing SAC actor loss using relabeled replay. All actor parameters
   are editable. Lower critics, temperature, batch statistics and sampled noise
   remain fixed in this short response. This is not three complete FlashSAC
   actor/critic/temperature updates, and is not the old ridge head fit.
6. Evaluate the learned wrench-cost critic on the adapted actor. Backpropagate
   through the three actor-learning steps AND reference observations into the
   upper actor. Four upper Adam steps are taken with a normalized correction
   radius of 0.25 (at most 5 mm wrist translation and 0.05 rad finger/rotation
   increment before additional cumulative/rate limits). Map the actor output
   smoothly into the feasible coefficient box; a neutral actor uses its midpoint.
   A joint at its lower limit therefore starts with a small inward correction,
   not a blocked outward correction. The zero-edit control is exactly zero.
7. Treat the short response ONLY as a proposal mechanism. For physical
   validation, start the edited and unchanged references from the SAME full
   incoming FlashSAC learner, including optimizer state, target critic,
   temperature, normalization statistics, and scheduler clocks. Neither starts
   from the three-step actor copy. Each branch gets fresh, isolated replay,
   fresh simulator experience, and the ordinary complete FlashSAC update.
8. Train at matched checkpoints. Requested floors are 50K / 100K / 200K aggregate
   environment transitions. The resolved budget is the larger of each floor
   and 1 / 2 / 4 reference horizons per world, rounded up to vector collections.
   Example: 401 reference frames and 2,048 worlds resolve to 819,200 / 1,638,400 /
   3,276,800 transitions PER branch. A 50K budget would cover only 25 frames per
   world and could never reach a later grasp from the fixed initial state.
   Check actual frame coverage too: a nominal horizon is not proof that early
   termination allowed the training worlds to reach the failure interval.
9. Hold the incoming lower reward weights and VOC stage fixed during the
   comparison. Use matching learning/reset seeds, exact original reset joints,
   the same optimizer settings and fresh replay warmup in both branches.
   Replay holds 262,144 transitions; learning starts at max(8,192, batch size).
   Collection uses the warm-started policy even during replay warmup.
   Evaluation at every checkpoint remains full-horizon, deterministic, VOC=0.
10. Train one unchanged-reference control through all checkpoints, then reuse
    that measured control curve for each window at the matching budget. Train
    each candidate continuously across checkpoints, keeping its physics state,
    replay and optimizer state. At most one extra vector training scene is
    alive at a time. The main training run remains paused and untouched.
11. Require two consecutive ready checkpoints with stable costs, per-object
    local pose errors, success and support errors, and a stable advantage sign.
    By default readiness requires at least 32 actor and 32 critic updates,
    nonzero parameter changes in both branches. The candidate also needs 128
    observed failure-region transitions and to reach that interval's final
    frame. An equally trained control may continue failing earlier; that is a
    valid comparison outcome, not a veto on a successful candidate. Its actual
    coverage is still recorded. Cost tolerance is 5% (unit floor),
    position tolerance 3 mm, orientation tolerance .03 rad, success tolerance
    .02 and support-error tolerance .05. Extend training while the comparison
    changes. At the maximum budget, unresolved comparisons are INCONCLUSIVE
    and rejected. These checks are empirical stability, NOT proven convergence.
12. Among stable comparisons, require local physical cost improvement over BOTH
    the matched trained control and incoming pair, plus existing per-object
    regression and archived-prefix guards. Publish the best passing reference
    together with ALL its tested lower learning state. Retain its Adam moments;
    reset only per-episode return accumulation because training restarts at
    frame zero. Clear main and auxiliary replay on publication. Rejection
    retains the original learner/reference/replay. Upper learning persists.

WHAT THE WRENCH LABEL MEANS

It is the existing CHORD-style object-centric contact wrench SUPPORT descriptor,
not a measured six-axis net force/torque vector. Per-frame costs use the squared
relative tolerance-band error (weight 5), missed support, and unintended support
energy. All hands participate, objects get equal weight, targets are immutable.
Termination incurs at least 20 cost; termination and finite-horizon truncation
stop TD bootstrapping. VOC/reset context is included in the cost critic input.

MATHEMATICAL SCOPE (PLAIN TEXT)

theta = 56 local reference coefficients; phi = full lower actor parameters.
phi_0 = current lower actor (constant at start of each upper round).
phi_{k+1}(theta) = Project(phi_k(theta) - inner_rate * grad_phi SAC_loss).
F_K(theta) = mean(Q_wrench(obs(theta), pi_phi_K(theta)(obs(theta))))
             + regularization * mean(theta^2).
The upper actor supplies theta, and autodiff computes the derivative of F_K.
Log the total gradient, the gradient with phi_K detached, and their difference.
That difference isolates the contribution through lower actor learning.

This is a finite-step unrolling approximation using a learned upper objective.
It is NOT an implicit-Hessian algorithm, stock upper FlashSAC, a certified
gradient of discontinuous physical contacts, or proof of optimal full RL.
The critic may be inaccurate; its held-out TD error does not certify action
gradients. Physical acceptance is mandatory. Native FK finite differences and
fixed replay distributions introduce additional explicit approximations.

Long adaptation is a validation stage, not part of the autodiff graph. Its
gradient is not claimed to equal the short response gradient. This change
provides evidence after meaningful training, rather than a convergence proof
or an exact hypergradient of fully trained neural FlashSAC.

RUN

Use the same container/dependencies and episode assets as the existing jobs.
In scripts/train_flash_sac.py select:

  +bilevel=wrench_hypergradient
  reset.fixed_initial_state=true
  reset.voc_decay_steps=0

Keep the existing final reference, meshes, masses, controller, reward and lower
training overrides. Remove old +bilevel.* overrides that conflict with the new
group. Do not change masses or reward weights just to enable this optimizer.
Use a fresh output directory. The inherited editor refuses resume because upper
state/replay restoration is not implemented; it must not silently reset them.

For a first GPU smoke run, retain the existing episode/assets/controller flags
and use these additional overrides (one upper round before the final step):

  scene.world_count=64
  training.total_environment_steps=131072
  training.replay.capacity=262144
  training.replay.minimum_size=8192
  training.replay.batch_size=1024
  training.checkpoint.save_interval_environment_steps=131072
  bilevel.warmup=65536
  bilevel.interval=65536

For episode_000001's current 335-frame, 20 Hz reference, adaptation at 64 worlds
uses 50,048 / 100,032 / 200,000 transitions per branch. At 2,048 worlds it uses
684,032 / 1,368,064 / 2,736,128. Full adaptation remains enabled in the smoke
recipe; this checks the actual long-training path rather than bypassing it.

OBSERVABILITY

hypergradient/gradient_norm, direct_gradient_norm, response_gradient_norm
hypergradient/predicted_wrench_return_gain, actual_local_cost_gain, accepted, physics_trials
hypergradient/critic_train_loss, critic_heldout_td_mae, replay_rows
reference_change/* : actual committed wrist cm and finger degrees
adaptation/environment_transitions, max_budget_per_branch, selected_budget,
stable_candidates

Predicted gain is discounted learned wrench-support cost plus edit penalty.
Actual gain is the physical local score (object pose, support, stability and edit
penalty); these quantities have different scales and must not be equated.

upper_updates.jsonl and search_batches/step_*/scores.json contain each window,
56D proposal, model prediction, physical result, and rejection reasons.
upper_state.msgpack stores the upper actor, twin critic, target and optimizer
states for inspection; selected_reference.npz records the published reference.
The existing demo archive continues to save the best validated policy/reference.
adaptation_progress.jsonl records each physical checkpoint as soon as it
finishes: branch, budget, learning updates, parameter movement, frame coverage,
VOC and physical metrics. Search records contain the complete paired learning
curves, stability decisions and rejection reasons. Side-training transitions
are reported separately from the main training clock; selected optimizer
clocks include the actual additional learning. At four windows the maximum
side work is five branches times the largest resolved budget, plus evaluations.

TESTS

test_wrench_hypergradient.py: independent analytic/finite-difference gradients,
nonzero learning-response contribution using production FlashSAC networks,
critic fitting, bounded proposals, reference-version isolation.
test_reference_relabel.py: quaternion frames, finger units, immutable fields,
native Sharpa FK at joint limits and feasible upper proposals.
test_wrench_hypergradient_editor.py: capture copies, support labels, terminal
handling, accepted/rejected publication and replay contract.
test_lower_adaptation.py: production FlashSAC actor AND critic training on fresh
experience, matched-seed reproducibility, source-state/donation isolation, full
learning-state publication, horizon-aware budgets, readiness, and comparison
extension or rejection.

Conceptual foundation: finite inner-optimization differentiation,
https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/franceschi18a.html . Its convergence conditions
are not asserted for this learned critic/contact-simulation implementation.

INITIAL SHORT-RESPONSE VALIDATION (2026-10-15, before long adaptation)

17 targeted CPU checks passed in 59.32 seconds inside the existing
flash_chord:mixed-ep01-media image. This includes the three retargeting test
files above, test_rejected_search_callback.py, and the real FlashSAC runner's
test_reference_capture_hooks_run_before_donation_and_publish_metrics.
The numerical quadratic test compares autodiff with an independent analytic
solution and finite differences; the production-network test verifies a
nonzero gradient through lower learning. Native Newton FK verifies finger
joint-limit handling. Simulator acceptance/publication tests use controlled
rollout results; they are not evidence of improved episode performance.

An additional broader runner suite had 28 passes and five CUDA-only failures
because this container was deliberately run on CPU. No full GPU training or
episode-level improvement has been evaluated for this new engine yet. Existing
jobs were neither modified nor restarted during this implementation.

LONG-ADAPTATION VALIDATION (2026-10-15)

30 targeted CPU checks passed in 72.00 seconds using the same image. The suite
adds test_lower_adaptation.py to the five-file selection above. It verifies
nonzero actor, critic, temperature and target-critic learning with the production
FlashSAC executable; exact matched-seed reproducibility; continued training
across checkpoints; full learning-state publication; and preservation of the
incoming learner/replay. It also tests extending unstable comparisons, rejecting
at the cap, guarding both objects, and rejecting insufficient frame coverage.
Configuration composition, native Sharpa FK, hypergradients and training hooks
also pass. Compilation and git diff whitespace checks pass. The full episode
GPU path has not been executed; no performance improvement is claimed yet.

A final 19-test adaptation/editor rerun passed in 31.32 seconds after adding
the early-failing-control regression case. It confirms that an unchanged
controller's failure to reach the scoring interval does not block a stable,
improving candidate that has enough actual training coverage there.
