Upper refinement audit — 2026-10-16

Assessment

The finite actor-response method can propose useful reference corrections, but
the available physics evidence does not establish that it does so reliably.
Correct gradients of a learned surrogate are not necessarily good gradients of
the physical task. This update fixes reproducible learning-signal errors; GPU
validation and accepted physical improvements remain outstanding.

Evidence from the October 15 full runs

Episode 1, seeds 43/44/45: 16 completed upper rounds, 56 measured proposals,
zero accepted corrections. All proposals predicted improvement, 34 improved
the equally adapted control, and 11 improved both that control and the incoming
pair. Every final proposal failed adaptation stability; the 11 local improvers
also violated other guards. These are results of the old full runs, not results
of the previously queued October 16 feedback implementation.

The median lower-response/direct gradient ratio was about 1.7e-5 to 2.3e-5.
Most of the proposed direction therefore came from changing reference inputs
with a frozen controller, rather than its modeled learning response.

Implemented corrections

1. Blend TD and measured-return targets before applying Huber loss. Separate
   clipped losses could exactly cancel while the measured return was badly
   underestimated. Complete-rollout calibration now defaults to MC weight 1.
2. Use cost_scale=.01 consistently for TD costs, MC returns and edit penalties.
   Keep raw and normalized diagnostic units explicit.
3. Calibrate on the current unassisted baseline before proposals. Outer states
   must come from this upper round, not old controllers under the same reference.
4. Differentiate three projected Adam actor updates with the incoming moments,
   schedule and configured learning-rate multipliers. Regularize the second
   moment square root with eps_root=1e-12 for finite higher derivatives.
5. Keep the upper target as measured full-horizon support error. Early tracking
   cutoffs retain stale reference timing in terminal observations, so omit those
   rows from upper TD fitting. Retain them for inner actor replay. Complete
   unassisted MC rollouts provide actual failed-suffix evidence. Lower controller
   termination semantics are unchanged; no invented terminal suffix cost is used.
6. Account for one frame of success-score resolution in stability checks.
   Existing pose, support, advantage and saved-prefix requirements remain.
7. Record actual proposals per window, baseline metrics and round identity.
   There is one learned 56D proposal per selected window; 2,048 is the number
   of lower training/adaptation worlds, not 2,048 upper proposals.

Limits and next decision

The three-step response freezes the lower critic, temperature, statistics and
sampled state distribution. Physics adaptation remains a separate acceptance
test. The shared cost critic is not conditioned on all lower-policy weights,
and measured-return accuracy alone does not certify its reference derivatives.

A synthetic CPU test with warm Adam moments increased the response-gradient
norm 34.2x versus plain SGD, but the response was still only 0.119% of the total.
With fresh zero moments it did not increase meaningfully. Neither test uses a
trained physical controller; these numbers do not predict task improvement.

The full runs should be judged by measured proposal/control improvement,
accepted wrist/finger changes, failure-prefix progress and actual videos.
Predicted critic gain or nonzero gradients alone are insufficient. Validation
retains the bounded physical budgets and tests every selected window; these
changes add no additional simulated rollouts.
